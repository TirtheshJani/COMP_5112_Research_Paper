# S2 simulation summary

All intervals are Wilson 95 percent intervals. In fig_puzzle.pdf the counts are pooled over blur, noise and tilt at each px per cell, with finger 0 and finger 1 shown as separate panels. Decoder "single" is one 5 x 5 window; decoder "grid" is the four-window decoder.

Prediction 1 (no read with at most one misread cell is wrong on a strong-form pattern, for either decoder): on the rendered puzzle with the single window decoder, 0 of 21436 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent) of the reads with at most one misread cell were wrong; on the rendered puzzle with the four windows decoder, 0 of 21436 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent) of the reads with at most one misread cell were wrong; on the dense tori, arm forced1, single-window decoder, 0 of 300 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 1.26 percent) of the reads with at most one misread cell were wrong; on the dense tori, arm noise, single-window decoder, 0 of 1244 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.31 percent) of the reads with at most one misread cell were wrong. The data agree with this prediction for every cell tested. The four-window decoder was not run on the dense tori, so that cell is untested.

Prediction 2 (on the dense tori, forced1 arm, the single-window decoder gives wrong reads on the control pattern and the four-window decoder rejects nearly all such reads on both patterns): in the forced1 arm with the single-window decoder, wrong reads on the orient (control) pattern were 67 of 300 (22.33 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 17.99 to 27.38 percent), and on the strong pattern 0 of 300 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 1.26 percent). The single-window half agrees: the control pattern produces wrong reads and the strong pattern does not. The four-window half was not run on the dense tori (the dense run used the single decoder only), so it is untested and nothing here supports or contradicts it.

Prediction 3 (on the rendered puzzle, wrong reads are rare for both patterns and both decoders; wrong over all reads in the whole sweep): strong, single window: 0 of 21600 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent); strong, four windows: 0 of 21600 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent); orient, single window: 0 of 21600 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent); orient, four windows: 0 of 21600 (0.00 percent, Wilson 95 percent interval 0.00 to 0.02 percent). Every pattern and decoder is under 1 percent, so the data agree that wrong reads are rare.
